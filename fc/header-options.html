1. Underside

The same 3x9 right-angle header soldered on the bottom of the lower board, pins forward.

Clearance
5.2 mm nominal
What changes
No board change, same Gerbers. Same holes and pin-to-net map; the rows are upside down seen from the front (signal lowest).
The catch
Hangs 7.5 mm below the lower board at the front; the frame must be open there.

2. 11 mm gap

Header on top of the lower board; boards moved 3 mm further apart.

Clearance
3.0 mm nominal
What changes
Standoffs (11 mm) and the upper board-to-board connector change.
The catch
Stack 3 mm taller; the new connector is not stocked by JLCPCB.

3. Cut tails

Header on top, 8 mm gap; the upper Dupont header solder tails are cut to 0.5 mm.

Clearance
0.9 mm (0.26 worst case) nominal
What changes
None.
The catch
Tight, and depends on trimming every board.

4. Two-row

A 2x9 right-angle header on top, in the two hole rows nearest the edge; 8 mm gap.

Clearance
2.5 mm nominal
What changes
Lower board re-routed.
The catch
No 5 V pins on this connector.

5. All Dupont headers on the upper board

The lower board carries no Dupont header. The upper board carries two 3-row right-angle Dupont headers on its top side: the present 3x12 at the front edge and a new 3x9 at the rear edge, pins pointing rearward, for M1 to M6, LED and the two companion-supply columns.

Clearance
1.6 mm nominal between the upper rear Dupont header solder tails (tips 6.6 mm above the lower board) and a 12 AWG battery/ESC wire joint on the lower board (assumed up to 5.0 mm high; 2.1 mm over the 4.5 mm insulation). With the same tolerance allowance as the other options (1.34 mm) about 0.3 mm remains, so the tails should be trimmed here too.
What changes
Both boards need new layouts and new Gerbers. On the upper board the rear strip (7.6 x 23.4 mm) is occupied: on top the microSD socket, the video overlay chip (AT7456E) and three to six small parts beside it; underneath the 27 MHz crystal and two capacitors; a standoff hole is at one corner. All of these have to move. The only free gap on top is 3 mm wide, so no narrower header fits without moves. The lower board loses the header, and the board-to-board connector pin assignment changes: the seven motor and LED pins are no longer needed, and the companion 5 V supply needs pins it does not have now.
The catch
A full re-layout of the upper board, which has little free area. The 3 A companion supply (and its ground return) must come up through the board-to-board connector: rated 2.9 A per pin, so at least two pins, better three, out of the seven freed. As drawn, the corner tail overlaps a standoff by about 0.3 mm, so the header must be narrower or shifted. The Dupont plugs sit directly above the battery and ESC wires.

Reference: as first designed (rejected)

3-row header on top of the lower board, 8 mm gap, upper Dupont header solder tails as supplied (1.4 mm). The tail tips are level with the tops of the lower Dupont header pins and 0.63 mm to the side of them.

About the pictures

The two boards, the upper Dupont header with its solder tails, the standoffs and the board-to-board connector are the project's own 3D models. The lower Dupont header, the Dupont plugs and wires are drawn from the header drawing's dimensions (0.64 mm pins at 1.21 / 3.75 / 6.29 mm, 7.5 mm body; 5.0 mm body for the two-row part). In option 1's side view the frame plate is drawn 2.5 mm below the lower board (grommet height); its thickness and where it ends are illustrative. In option 2 the top 3 mm of each standoff and the taller board-to-board connector are drawn stand-ins. In option 3 the model's tails are clipped at 0.5 mm. In the front views the lower Dupont header's plastic body is drawn transparent so the pins and tails behind it can be seen. In option 5 the upper rear Dupont header, the 12 AWG wires (4.5 mm over insulation) and their solder joints (5.0 mm high) are drawn stand-ins on the unchanged boards, so the parts the header would displace are still shown. Side and front views are parallel projections, to scale.
