StackF765: pre-fabrication verification plan

Note: this plan was written against revision B; the boards are now revision C (both Dupont headers on the upper board, none on the lower board).

Summary

This is a plan of checks still to do before the boards are ordered. As of 2026-11-25 none of them has been run. The risks below are one reviewer’s reading of the design files; they are not confirmed and some may turn out to be wrong.

Main risks

  1. Plugging in USB may briefly push the USB supply to about 7 V, above the 6 V limit of the chip that selects USB or battery power.
  2. The on/off (“enable”) pins of both 5 V regulators are left unconnected. Moisture or solder residue could switch 5 V off in flight.
  3. The 5 V regulators’ safety margins have never been simulated; their output can already reach the 5.25 V limit of the video-overlay chip.
  4. Next to the current-measuring resistor, the 50 A battery current runs through the top copper layer only, so it could run hot.
  5. The motion sensor sits 2.35 mm from an unsupported board edge, which may add vibration to its readings. Not fixable once boards are made.

Do first, before paying for boards (about 7 hours)

  1. Simulate plugging in USB and choose a fix for the voltage spike. 1.5 h
  2. Simulate connecting the battery and the start-up current surge. 1.5 h
  3. Decide the fix for the unconnected enable pins (two resistors per regulator). 1 h plus the design change
  4. Regenerate the manufacturing files, re-run the design-rule check and compare with the files to be ordered. 1 h
  5. Run the board maker’s own manufacturing and assembly check on the exact order files (the owner uploads them). 1.5 h

Then

  • Also before ordering (13–18 hours): three longer analyses that put numbers on risks 3, 4 and 5.
  • Before first power-up (about 12 hours): inspect the boards, measure them unpowered, build a test fixture and test program.
  • With hardware (about 26 hours, plus test flights): bench tests of power-up, heat at 50 A, sensor calibration, vibration, interference and temperature.

1. Executive summary

The existing checks prove the files are self-consistent: schematic = model = board = Gerbers, the pin map matches the firmware, the boards fit, firmware builds. Two paper reviews and a pin-by-pin datasheet audit found no wiring error. What nobody has done is simulate anything, solve any field, or model any heat or vibration. Every analogue and power number in the repository is a closed-form hand calculation. research/audit-2026-11-19.md section 3 says so itself: "ngspice is not installed; these are closed-form results".

That statement is out of date in one useful way: the ngspice 42 shared library ships with KiCad on this server and runs headless from Python (checked). So most of the circuit simulation below needs no installation.

Top five risks (evidence in section 2):

  1. USB hot-plug overshoot on VBUS exceeds the 6 V absolute maximum of the supply selector U5. Known, documented, left unfixed. Likely peak about 7 V [inference].
  2. Both 5 V regulators have floating enable pins (megohm nodes, 0.5 mm from the battery pin). Leakage from flux or condensation can switch the flight controller's supply off in flight. Raised in the first review, never actioned.
  3. Regulator loop and transient margins are unverified with a smaller inductor (4.7 µH) and less output capacitance (17–21 µF effective) than TI's 5 V design, and the set-point tolerance already reaches the OSD chip's 5.25 V limit.
  4. The 50 A path uses only the top layer between each wire pad and the shunt. The inner and bottom copper is a dead end there because there are no vias at the shunt pads. The documents say four layers. About 4–5 W ends up in the lower board under the upper board, with no thermal model.
  5. The IMU sits 2.35 mm from the board edge on the unsupported front part of the upper board, between the standoffs and a 36-pin header that carries the cable loads. No modal or strain estimate exists.

First five things to do, ranked by risk avoided per hour (all before paying; about 7 hours):

  1. SPICE the USB hot-plug into U5 with ngspice and pick a damping fix. 1.5 h.
  2. SPICE the battery hot-plug and inrush on the lower board. 1.5 h.
  3. Decide the enable-pin fix for the two regulators (a two-resistor divider each). 1 h plus the change.
  4. Refill zones on a copy, re-run DRC, re-export and diff all fab files including the IPC-D-356 netlist. 1 h.
  5. Run the fab's DFM/DFA check on the exact files to be ordered (the owner uploads). 1.5 h.

Then the three longer analyses that put numbers on risks 3, 4 and 5 (13–18 hours): TI's model of the TPS62933 for stability and load steps; a DC current-density and voltage-drop solve of the 50 A path; a vibration-mode and strain estimate at the IMU.

Honest limits. PDN impedance to hundreds of MHz, radiated emissions, and 3D thermal with airflow need commercial solvers (Ansys SIwave/Icepak, Cadence Sigrity/Celsius, Siemens HyperLynx/Flotherm, Keysight PIPro) or a week of open-source FEM work that would have to be installed first. For a 12 Mbit/s USB, 16 MHz SPI, 25–50 MHz SD board this is not where the respin risk is. Measure those on hardware instead. Nothing in this plan replaces the bench.

2. Top five risks, with evidence

Risk 1. VBUS hot-plug overshoot into U5 (LM66200), upper board

  • gen/upper.py:53-57: U5 VIN2 = VBUS; C2 1 µF 0402 and D3 (tvs5 = SMF5.0CA) on VBUS. gen/service.py:30: C1 1 µF. Total about 2 µF nominal, about 1 µF at 5 V bias [inference from the Samsung 0402 bias curve quoted in the audit].
  • research/audit-2026-11-19.md finding 9: D3 breaks down at 6.4 V minimum and clamps at 9.2 V; LM66200 absolute maximum is 6 V. research/design-review.md S5 says the same. docs/verification.md records it as "Not changed; do not use chargers that overshoot".
  • [inference] The overshoot does not need a bad charger. A 1 m USB cable plus the 100 mm JST-SH lead is roughly 0.5–1 µH and 0.2–0.5 Ω in series with 1–2 µF: characteristic impedance about 0.7 Ω, damping ratio 0.15–0.35, overshoot 35–60 %, i.e. 6.8–8 V before D3 starts to conduct. D3 then holds it somewhere between 6.4 and about 7.5 V. That is above the absolute maximum on every plug-in.
  • Consequence: latent or immediate damage to U5, the single part every supply current passes through.
  • Cheap fixes to evaluate in simulation: 0.5–1 Ω (or a ferrite) in series with VBUS at J5; a 4.7–10 µF capacitor with ESR; a unidirectional 5 V clamp with lower dynamic resistance.

Risk 2. Floating EN (and RT) pins on both TPS62933 regulators, lower board

  • gen/lower.py:38-39: nc=("EN", "RT"). [measured] U1 pad 2 unconnected-(U1-EN-Pad2) at (−3.34, 8.15), pad 3 VESC at (−3.34, 8.65): 0.5 mm pitch, 0.2 mm gap, battery voltage next door. Same on U2.
  • research/design-review.md N1: internal pull-up is about 0.7 µA; EN absolute maximum is 6 V; "flux residue or condensation leaking > ~1 µA to ground would shut the 5 V rail off in flight". It was filed as a note, and the generator still leaves the pin open.
  • [inference] Leakage can go either way: to ground (rail off) or to the adjacent VIN pad (EN pulled towards 8–12 V, above its 6 V rating). A multirotor sees condensation.
  • U1 feeds the flight controller. Loss of +5V in flight is loss of the aircraft.
  • Related: gen/lower.py:23 puts the servo-rail pins of J2 columns 1–7 on the flight controller's own +5V. A stalled servo or a shorted lead takes the MCU supply with it.

Risk 3. Regulator stability, transient and set-point margins, lower board

  • gen/lower.py:46-51: 4.7 µH, 51 k + 2.2 k / 10 k, two 22 µF 0805 output capacitors, no feed-forward capacitor.
  • research/audit-2026-11-19.md findings 6, 17 and section 3.1: output capacitance 21 µF effective (17 µF with tolerance), "at or below" TI's 20 µF typical; TI's 5 V/500 kHz design uses 6.8 µH; a 1.5 A step "moves the output a little more than 5 %"; set-point range with tolerances 4.87–5.25 V; "The AT7456E maximum is 5.25 V ... inside, with no margin at the very top".
  • No loop-gain or time-domain simulation exists. Loads are unknown: long servo leads, a companion computer with hundreds of µF, an ESC BEC back-feeding.
  • [inference] On a full-load release the energy in the inductor lifts the output by roughly √(V² + L·I²/C) − V ≈ 0.3–0.4 V with 17 µF, on top of a set-point that may already be 5.25 V. That reaches about 5.6 V on +5V, which feeds U5 (5.5 V recommended maximum) and through it the OSD chip.

Risk 4. 50 A path and lower-board heat

  • [measured] Net VBAT has no vias apart from the twelve 0.4 mm holes inside wire pad J10 (y = 20.5 … 23.0). Shunt R1 pad 1 is a top-layer pad 1.8 × 3.4 mm at (−10.1, 16.6). The VBAT high current zone exists on all four layers (gen/lower.py:210) but the inner and bottom copper has no connection at the shunt end. The same is true on the VESC side: the six VESC vias are at the regulators and the TVS, none at R1 pad 2 (−4.9, 16.6).
  • So all 50 A crosses about 1.5 mm of top-layer 2 oz copper and enters a 1.8 mm-wide pad. docs/design.md ("5–6 mm wide on four layers") and research/layout-review.md ("solid zone on all four layers ... under 0.1 mΩ") do not describe that.
  • [inference] Sheet resistance of 70 µm copper is about 0.25 mΩ per square at 20 °C, 0.31 at 85 °C. Expect 0.1–0.2 mΩ and 0.25–0.5 W per side at 50 A, concentrated at the pad edges, next to a shunt that is itself dissipating 1.25 W. Adding the two regulators at load (0.8 W + 0.45 W each, audit 3.1) gives 4–5 W in a 31.5 × 48 mm board that has another board 8 mm above it.
  • [measured] D1 (the input clamp) reaches the VESC plane through 0.2 mm track and a single 0.3 mm via at (3.3, 15.15). Adequate for short pulses [inference]; a second via costs nothing.
  • [measured] The whole lower board has 11 ground vias outside the wire pads.

Risk 5. IMU placement and stack mechanics

  • [measured] U2 (ICM-45686) is at (13.4, −11.3) on the upper board: 2.35 mm from the right edge, 12.7 mm from the front edge. Standoffs are at (−12.3, −3.0), (12.3, −3.0), (12.3, 15.9) (gen/lower.py:32): three, not four, and none forward of y = −3. The 3 × 12 header J1 has its hole rows at y = −17.7 … −22.8 with the body hanging off the front edge.
  • So the IMU sits on a 21 mm overhang, between the support line and the connector that takes every cable force. The fourth corner (rear left, beside the microSD socket) is unsupported.
  • [measured] IMU_SCK runs on In2.Cu within 1.6 mm of the package centre. TDK's layout notes (AN-000393, AN-000262 [web: titles only, not opened]) ask for no vias or tracks under the package.
  • [inference] First bending mode of the overhang with the header mass is somewhere between 0.6 and 2 kHz; motor and blade-pass harmonics of a small multirotor reach that band. Cable pull on the header strains the board at the IMU and shifts gyro and accelerometer bias.
  • [inference] The grommet isolation of the stack is partly bypassed by four 12 AWG power wires and up to 27 soldered signal wires on the lower board.
  • This cannot be fixed after fabrication, which is why it is in the top five even though it may turn out acceptable.

Vibration (J11)

  1. Tap test on the bench with INS_LOG_BAT_* batch sampling: find the first resonance of the upper board. Compare with G1.
  2. Motors on the airframe, props off then on, throttle sweep: FFT of gyro and accelerometer; VIBE clipping counters.
  3. Repeat with the header cables tied down and loose, and with the power leads stiff and strain-relieved.

Criteria: clipping count 0; VIBE below 30 m/s² (ArduPilot's own guidance [recall]); no resonance peak within the motor fundamental band at hover; noise floor not worse than a known-good soft-mounted board on the same frame.

Flight progression (J15)

  1. Bench, props off: motor order and direction, DShot and telemetry, failsafes, battery monitor against a meter.
  2. Tethered or hand-held hover in Stabilize for 30 s; land; read the log (vibration, rail events, current calibration, IMU consistency).
  3. Hover in AltHold then Loiter with GPS and compass; compass-motor interference check.
  4. Five-minute hover with temperature logging; check the IMU temperature and the lower-board hot spots straight after landing.
  5. Step up throttle demands in stages (50, 75, 100 %) watching for resets; only then full missions.
  6. Repeat step 5 on a 3S pack at full charge, where the input clamp and the regulators have the least margin.

A second airframe-independent board should stay on the bench as a reference for comparisons.
